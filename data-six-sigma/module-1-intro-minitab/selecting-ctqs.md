# Selecting CTQs

## The Data Collection Challenge

You're ready to start your project with an ambitious objective—solving that one recurring problem everyone complains about. The next step is to collect data and analyze.

**But what data should you collect?**

This can be a difficult question. Which aspect should you measure?

| Project Objective | Measurement Challenge |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| Increase customer satisfaction | Not immediately clear what to measure |
| Prevent product failures | Which failure modes? Which metrics? |
| Increase healthcare accessibility | What defines "accessible"? |

---

## Operationalizing Your Problem

In the DMAIC phases, this is part of the **Measure phase**—making the problem quantifiable and measurable.

**Operationalization** = Selecting measurable characteristics that reflect your project objective

> You need process metrics that are relevant for your project objectives.

---

## What is a CTQ?

**CTQ = Critical to Quality characteristic**

A CTQ is a property of a product or service that is **relevant to your project objective**.

### How to Find Your CTQs

Ask yourself:
1. What is the metric I want to change?
2. Will my project be successful if I change that metric?

### Alternative Terminology

| Field | Term |
|-------|------|
| Lean Six Sigma | CTQ |
| Statistics | Y-variable |
| Research | Dependent variable |
| General | Outcome variable |

---

## Example: Waffle Factory

**Situation:** A factory produces waffles. Each morning, production must be set up. Problem: Monday morning set-up takes longer than other days, and the process runs worse for the rest of the day.

**Project Goal:** Find what causes this phenomenon and improve the process so Monday set-up equals other days.

### Identifying the CTQs

| Aspect to Improve | What "Worse" Means | Possible CTQs |
|-------------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Set-up takes more time | Longer duration | Duration of set-up, Start time of production |
| Process runs worse | More scrap produced | Amount of scrap, Total production volume |

---

## Example: Insurance Address Changes

**Situation:** Processing address changes takes too much time—both for the business and the customer.

### Identifying the CTQs

| Aspect | CTQ | Unit |
|--------|-----|------|
| Employee time | Processing time per address change | Minutes |
| Customer time | Throughput time (form completion + waiting + processing) | Days |

> In most service processes, processing time is measured in **minutes**, whereas throughput time is often measured in **days**.

---

## Example: Sales Department

**Situation:** Sales representatives visit potential customers, investigate needs, write offers, and follow up on questions/complaints from existing customers.

**Management Concerns:**
- Revenues are too low
- Representatives visit too few clients
- Work pressure is too high
- Representatives feel overburdened by administrative tasks

### Identifying the CTQs

| Project Objective | Possible CTQs |
|-------------------|---------------|
| Increase revenues | Number of visits, Conversion rate of offers |
| Lower work pressure | Time spent with new customers, Time spent with existing customers, Time spent on administrative tasks |

> There are often multiple ways to select your CTQs. Always be critical and check whether they capture the project objective.

---

## Summary

- **CTQ** (Critical to Quality) is a property of a process, product, or service relevant to your project objective
- Find CTQs by asking: **"What would I want to change?"**
- CTQs are also called: Y-variable, dependent variable, outcome variable
- **Operationalization** is the process of making objectives measurable
- There are often **multiple valid CTQs** for a single objective—be critical in your selection
