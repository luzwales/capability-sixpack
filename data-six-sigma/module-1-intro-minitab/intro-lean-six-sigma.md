# Introduction to Lean Six Sigma

## The Power of Data-Driven Improvement

A project leader presents results to 20 participants. His graph shows **machine effectiveness measured in percentages**, with each dot representing one week:

| Period | Effectiveness | Description |
|--------|---------------|-------------|
| Before project | 68% average/week | Original process baseline |
| After improvements | 78% average/week | Post-implementation results |

This is a typical **Lean Six Sigma project presentation**—a data-driven approach to process improvement.

---

## What is a Process?

**Processes are a series of actions or steps taken to make a product or deliver a service.**

### Examples Across Industries

| Domain | Example Process |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Industry** | Encapsulation process of medicine (putting medicine into capsules) |
| **Healthcare** | Treatment of patients in a hospital |
| **Services** | Handling phone calls in a call center |

> Every organization can be seen as a collection of processes.

---

## Why Improve Processes?

Organizations invest considerable time in process improvement for several reasons:

- **Competition** — To withstand competitive pressure
- **Customer expectations** — To adapt to rising customer demands
- **Technological innovation** — To leverage new capabilities

### Improvement Examples by Industry

| Domain | Improvement Direction |
|--------|----------------------|
| **Encapsulation (Industry)** | Produce more capsules per hour → increase sales |
| **Hospital (Healthcare)** | Decrease occurrence of medical errors |
| **Call Center (Services)** | Increase customer satisfaction by lowering waiting times; reduce personnel cost by lowering handling time (or both) |

---

## What is Lean Six Sigma?

A **stepwise method for running a process improvement project**.

### Main Features

- **Evidence-based** approach
- **Uses process data** to drive decisions

---

## How Data is Used in Projects

Data can analyze several aspects of your process:

### 1. Finding the Biggest Problems

Data helps identify where to focus your project.

**Example (Encapsulation Process):**
Analysis of major production stops showed that from all possible stop reasons, **"waiting for gelatin"** consumed the most stop time. This knowledge focuses the project on the biggest problem.

### 2. Analyzing the Current Process

Understanding the baseline before making changes.

**Example (Hospital):**
A project aims to reduce diagnostic tests used. Before changes, you need to know:
- How many tests are we using right now?
- What is the average?
- Is there a lot of variation?

### 3. Investigating Improvement Effects

Measuring whether changes actually work.

**Example (Call Center):**
Test whether handling time is influenced by giving employees a certain training or not.

---

## Summary

- **Process improvement** is important to organizations
- **Lean Six Sigma** is a popular method for process improvement
- **Data is critical** when improving processes
- Data helps you:
  - Find the biggest problems
  - Diagnose current processes
  - Investigate the effects of improvement actions
